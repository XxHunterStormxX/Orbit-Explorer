# Gameplay and Systems

## Setting

The player manages **Base Ares Planitia**, a fictional human settlement on Mars. The base is a game scenario, not an existing NASA facility or a proposed operational mission site.

## Player responsibilities

The player monitors the colony and makes decisions to maintain its resources, support the crew and develop infrastructure.

## Resources and systems represented

The prototype includes game systems for:

- **Food:** supports the crew; shortages can harm colony status.
- **Water:** a life-support resource consumed by the crew.
- **Oxygen:** a life-support resource consumed by the crew.
- **Energy:** supports base operations and infrastructure.
- **Science:** used to acquire or develop improvements.
- **Crew/population:** astronauts contribute to science generation while increasing resource consumption.
- **Health and morale:** indicators of colony condition.

The numerical rates and effects are balancing values created for gameplay. They should not be interpreted as validated life-support requirements for Mars.

## Construction and progression

The prototype includes buildable or upgradeable systems such as life-support improvements, greenhouse/food production, energy generation, habitat capacity, protection and research-related upgrades. Costs and effects are game mechanics.

## Difficulty modes

The interface provides two modes:

- **Explorer:** a more accessible mode presented for younger players.
- **Astronaut:** a more demanding/normal mode.

## Events

The game includes simulated events such as solar/space-weather scenarios, dust storms, operational disruptions and crew-related events. These are game scenarios. They are not live NASA alerts and are not triggered automatically by the DONKI data panel.

## Player feedback

The interface uses resource indicators, alerts, status displays and event responses to show the consequences of decisions.

## Controls

The game is played through its on-screen buttons and interface panels in a modern web browser. The prototype is delivered as a single HTML file.
